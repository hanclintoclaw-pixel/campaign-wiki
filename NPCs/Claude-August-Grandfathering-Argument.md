## Transcript

**CONFEDERATED AMERICAN STATES**  
**Tribunal of Civic Status and Commercial Capacity**  
**In re Claude August and The Princeps Group**  
**Docket CS–2064–118 — Oral argument, 18 October 2064**

**Panel:** Presiding Adjudicator Helena Rusk; Adjudicators Jonas Voss and Celia Morrow.  
**Appearances:** Mara Ellery for August and Princeps; Deputy Public Advocate Thomas Keene for the Confederation.

[August sits beside counsel in human form: a teenage-looking male in a precisely fitted suit. He places a Princeps pen parallel to counsel’s papers.]

**RUSK:** Ms. Ellery. The question is ownership capacity, not whether Huntsville benefits from your client’s laboratories.

**ELLERY:** Agreed. I shall keep three things separate: residence, status, and the later acquisition of shares.

**VOSS:** You will also keep citizenship separate from the government’s willingness to tolerate a useful irregularity.

**ELLERY:** Certainly. Tolerance is revocable. A vested legal status is not tolerance.

**KEENE:** That assumes the very status she must prove.

**ELLERY:** Yes. I propose to prove it.

**RUSK:** Proceed.

**ELLERY:** First, the person now using Claude August was resident within the territory before and continuously through the Confederation’s founding in 2021. Exhibit A–1, admitted below, is the Limestone District subsurface survey of 14 September 2018. It records an occupied chamber complex, its resident’s identifying physical characteristics, and the resident’s declared name: Gameplayer.

**VOSS:** A dragon’s hoard is not a household registration.

**ELLERY:** No. It proves an inhabited place, a particular inhabitant, and an intention to remain. Those are residence facts. I do not offer the survey as a passport.

**MORROW:** Continuity?

**ELLERY:** Exhibit A–2: the district’s occupation-and-assessment return, certified 4 January 2022. Its attached inspection entries span 2020 and 2021. The same occupant maintained the chambers, paid the assessments, and claimed that address throughout the transition.

**KEENE:** Inspectors did not stand outside the entrance every hour. Nor does payment prove who slept there.

**ELLERY:** Both true. Continuous residence does not require continuous observation or continuous bodily presence. The records establish a maintained home. The Confederation offers no competing domicile, abandonment, or intervening removal.

**VOSS:** Your client was collecting territorial paperwork before our state existed. How industrious.

[August begins to smile. Ellery puts one finger on the margin of her notes. He becomes still.]

**ELLERY:** Industriousness is not a disqualification, Adjudicator.

**RUSK:** Identity remains contested.

**ELLERY:** Mr. August can confirm the connection orally.

**MORROW:** Or by dragonspeech? We will not have an answer every living person within sight can receive but the recording cannot preserve.

**ELLERY:** Ordinary audible speech only. Dragonspeech reaches living recipients in line of sight; microphones and recording devices do not receive it. A private impression cannot supply a reviewable answer.

**RUSK:** Mr. August, speak aloud. Do not supplement your answer by another means.

**AUGUST:** I understand. I am the resident identified as Gameplayer in those exhibits. Claude August is the name under which I conduct corporate affairs.

**CLERK:** Audible answer captured.

**KEENE:** We preserve our objection to identity proof by an interested party’s assertion.

**RUSK:** Preserved. Species remains assumed for this application, not adjudicated.

**ELLERY:** Second: residence at founding had legal consequences. Article Seven of the Articles of Confederated Settlement continued the civil personality and property capacity of settled territorial residents, subject to individual adjudication.

**VOSS:** “Residents.” Not “every creature occupying a hole.”

**ELLERY:** The article says residents. Its exclusion schedule names hostile military occupants and persons under specified civil disabilities. It does not exclude dragons.

**VOSS:** An omission does not make this boy a citizen.

**ELLERY:** I concede the article alone does not establish voting rights, eligibility for office, or every incident of citizenship. It does establish the civil personality necessary to hold property. Full recognition is our primary claim. Proprietary capacity is the narrower ground sufficient here.

**MORROW:** You are separating citizenship from ownership.

**ELLERY:** Where necessary. The state cannot defeat an expressly preserved property capacity merely by leaving a citizenship application unanswered.

**KEENE:** Counsel wants the benefits of membership without proving membership.

**ELLERY:** Counsel wants the consequences the founding instrument attached to residence. Membership may require more. The text requires no less.

**RUSK:** The later prohibition?

**ELLERY:** Third: section 12 of the Domestic Corporate Ownership Act, enacted in 2032, bars non-human controlling ownership. Section 31 preserves capacities vested under the founding settlement. The state reads that saving clause as protecting only particular assets already held.

**VOSS:** Princeps was not your client’s corporation at founding.

**ELLERY:** Correct. I do not suggest otherwise.

**VOSS:** Then stop calling him a pre-existing owner.

**ELLERY:** A pre-existing legal person with an existing capacity to own. The shares came later. The statute preserves “capacities,” not merely holdings. In *Petition of Bellwether Holdings*, 17 C.S.C. 204, this tribunal distinguished a preserved capacity from the particular property subsequently acquired through it.

**KEENE:** Bellwether concerned a corporate charter, not a dragon demanding citizenship.

**ELLERY:** Correct again. It governs the saving clause’s grammar, not my client’s species. The Articles supply his status. Bellwether prevents the state from replacing “capacities” with “assets.”

**VOSS:** The youngest great dragon arrives last and expects the oldest privileges.

[August turns his pen once between two fingers.]

**AUGUST:** Preserved privileges are—

**ELLERY:** My client will allow counsel to answer.

**RUSK:** He will.

**ELLERY:** His relative youth neither establishes nor defeats residence in 2021. Nor does maturity appear in the saving clause.

**RUSK:** Your fourth point.

**ELLERY:** The government-backed stock takeover. Exhibit A–3 is the Treasury participation schedule executed 22 June 2061. It identifies August as acquiring controlling beneficial ownership of Princeps and records the government’s participation after the financial scandal.

**KEENE:** Government support for a transaction cannot repeal an ownership statute. Estoppel cannot authorize what the legislature forbids.

**ELLERY:** Agreed. We do not ask it to. The preserved capacity makes the acquisition lawful. Estoppel prevents the government from denying the factual and legal position on which it deliberately procured that acquisition.

**KEENE:** Strategic necessity is not citizenship.

**ELLERY:** Nor is it a license to manufacture an owner for procurement purposes and disown him when title becomes inconvenient.

**VOSS:** There is also a public account involving dead parents and an inheritance.

**ELLERY:** There is. I neither reconcile nor rely upon that account. The executed stock schedule is the instrument before you.

**MORROW:** What exactly is the fatal admission?

**ELLERY:** That the state selected August as the beneficial owner, not merely a manager or temporary custodian. It expressly relied on his ownership to secure Princeps as a domestic military-research supplier. That is fatal to its present assertion that his ownership was never examined or accepted. It is not, standing alone, a substitute for the founding instrument.

**KEENE:** The applicant has also been identified as Rex Gainax. We are asked to let a succession of names carry one conveniently permanent entitlement.

**ELLERY:** Names change. Identity must be proved. That is why I began with evidence rather than reputation.

**RUSK:** Suppose we accept residence but decline immediate citizenship recognition.

**ELLERY:** Then preserve the shares while requiring status registration. Alternatively, place disputed voting powers with an approved trustee, leaving beneficial title intact, pending a defined determination. If the panel finds a licensing defect, impose conditional licensure and a filing deadline.

**VOSS:** He keeps the economic prize while others exercise restraint for him.

**ELLERY:** He bears the restrictions the panel lawfully imposes. The state retains a regulated domestic corporation. Neither outcome requires confiscation by ambiguity.

[August opens his mouth, then closes it.]

**RUSK:** Your requested order, precisely.

**ELLERY:** Recognize August’s founding-resident status and resulting citizenship; alternatively, declare his preserved civil personality and proprietary capacity sufficient to own Princeps. Reject retroactive application of section 12 to that vested capacity and dismiss the petition to void his controlling interest. If registration remains deficient, preserve beneficial ownership under supervised conditions until it is cured.

**RUSK:** Submitted.
