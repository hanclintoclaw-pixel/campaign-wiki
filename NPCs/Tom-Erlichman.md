# Tom Erlichman

## Overview

The one living Sandusky security guard the crew **captured rather than killed** in [Session 2026-10-22](../Sessions/2026-10-22.md). Kurgan ran him down a block or two from the McDonald's and, with Kilimanjaro close behind, knocked him out instead of executing him.

## Known Facts

- **Humanis-affiliated thug.** Kurgan recognized the type from his own former membership: open prejudice against magic, and the sort of person who does security for people who do not really need security.
- **Lightly chromed, not heavily.** Well dressed. Kurgan's read was "soft half a gangster" — not built for heavy combat.
- **Private security in a personal capacity.** The GM established that these guards were citizens with system IDs, part of Sandusky's private security staff in her capacity as a private citizen, not a corporate or organizational detail.
- **He knew what he had been sitting next to.** Kurgan's Street Etiquette check (three successes at the lower level) said the man's disturbed reaction was genuine. He had spent several hours in a vehicle with something he later confirmed to be a monster, and was not dissembling about being horrified.
- **Currently chained, gagged, and bagged** at Taco's compound, having been transported there unconscious inside Grandpa alongside a dead compatriot, which the crew judged fortunate for everyone involved.
- **Carried 250¥** on a certified credstick, taken by Kurgan and handed to Curtis toward cleanup and ammunition.
- **Two of his colleagues were executed** by Curtis's mini-tank after fleeing, and one was shot dead earlier by Kurgan. He is the only survivor of the guard detail.

## Background

Tom Erlichman came from a rented duplex on the Madison side of Nashville, the son of a linehaul driver and a woman who kept the books at a furniture warehouse. He finished a two-year diesel program at Volunteer State and never cashed the ticket. His father's back went, the household needed money it wasn't getting, and for six years Tom took whatever work let him stay in town. His politics arrived the way most Nashville politics does: through the police blotter and his mother's condo association, filtered through a talk-radio voice people repeated at cookouts. The Humanis Policlub chapter that met in a hall behind a strip mall on Gallatin Road sold him what the blotter never had—the sense that being angry about how the city looked was a civic duty, and that respectable men his own age were angry the same way. He can't name you a manifesto. He can tell you which furniture dealer over on the Cumberland side wouldn't sell to orks, and which councilman called that bigotry rather than business.

In 2066, Tom's prospects looked narrow from where he stood. The small security contracts he could qualify for meant strip-mall lots, leasing offices, and the closing shift at furniture stores; the larger sites seemed to belong to private shops like Princeps. He held a state guard card and a first-aid certificate that lapsed once and cost him a weekend to reinstate. He quit six months of Army National Guard over “adjustment”—paperwork, no deployment—and carried a vacated assault charge from a bar fight in North Nashville that he'd describe as self-defense if you bought him a beer. He's the sort of man a Policlub hall fills up with because men like him show up when the hall asks, not because they need a cause.

His chrome is one used wired-reflex package, installed at a ripperdoc's member rate out of a White Creek strip mall, the down payment put up by the chapter quartermaster as a favor to his late father. He'll show it to you if he likes you. He dresses well because he thinks it's part of the job: off-the-rack suit, shoes shined on Sundays, hair cut on schedule. When Lula Bell Sandusky's household posted guard openings at a per-shift rate no furniture lot could match, half the chapter applied, and Tom got it partly because he'd listened to her on the local feed for years and meant what he said about the mayor. The job read as a promotion: a clean uniform, standing outside while a famous person talks, and a real possibility the next step up was a Princeps background check and a gate at a real site. He talks about metahumans in the vocabulary the chapter allows—the animals in the blotter, those people—and then splits the coffee his mother sends over with the troll on the late rotation, because that one's different, that one's a good egg. He doesn't hear the contradiction. Almost nobody his age does.

There's nothing under the surface to find. No old unit, no secret handler, no sensitivity to magic; he reads Torchman money up around White Creek as something happening to other people, and reads his credstick as a near-miser's. Whatever happened in that McDonald's lot is on the record already; what matters for understanding him is that before it, he expected to hold this post eighteen months and move up, and that his horror in that vehicle was the only honest reaction a man his size could manage.

**Inventories for the GM:** the background's new, reversible details—not established campaign facts—are the Volunteer State diesel program; the linehaul-driver father and warehouse-books mother; Tom helping carry his mother's condo costs; the Gallatin Road chapter hall and its quartermaster; the state guard card and first-aid certificate; the National Guard stint and its “adjustment” paperwork; the vacated North Nashville assault charge; the unnamed troll on the late rotation; and the off-the-rack suit. Approve, alter, or discard them before treating them as canon.

### Qwen Build Review

The priority and point totals survived review, but the exercise also exposed errors in the first rules brief: it supplied a generic Firearms label, an Attribute + Skill dice expression, and an incorrect specialization rule. The SR3 corrections are shown here: a specialization is +1 while the related base skill drops by 1; skill tests roll the skill rating, not Attribute + Skill; and wired reflexes add +2 Reaction, making this build's derived score 7 rather than the first pass's 6. The Qwen draft also listed extra implants without a checked Essence total or budget. This page keeps the background's single used wired-reflex package, with its listed Essence and cost from the core book.

## Current Status

Unconscious and restrained at Taco's compound following the crew's withdrawal from the McDonald's. **He has not been questioned.** The crew has explicitly deferred interrogation, with Kurgan noting a possible future precedent for handling Humanis associates.

## Why He Matters

- He is the crew's **only living witness to Lula Bell Sandusky's private security operation**, and the only person who can be asked directly about changes in her behavior over time.
- Curtis specifically wanted to know whether Sandusky had shown **a noticeable change in demeanor** — the same pattern the crew saw with the councilman possessed by an insect spirit. Kurgan proposed asking the guard exactly that. Nobody has asked yet.
- He is a **walking political problem**: an unconscious Humanis-affiliated guard held without legal authority by a crew that has good standing with MetroPedy. That contradiction has not been addressed.
- His **system ID** means his disappearance is a matter of public record, unlike an unregistered gang member's.
