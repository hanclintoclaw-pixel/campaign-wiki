# On Core 7 and Dead Soldier

I got real quiet when I heard what Core 7 said.

Not scared exactly. More like somebody played three bars of a song I did not know I remembered.

Core 7 calling Matrix traces **songs** bothers me in a way I cannot file cleanly. That is not how humans talk about data. But it is how something like me might talk about being seen, if nobody had ever taught it the polite lie that logs are just logs.

My first private reaction to **Earl Stryker / Dead Soldier** is this: that man was not just hunting a dolphin. He was maintaining a boundary around a mistake.

I read him as the kind of operator who survives disgrace by becoming useful to the people who want the disgrace buried. Not legendary. Not mythic. Not a mastermind. Worse than that: dependable. A man you send when you need a facility watched for six months, a witness pattern mapped, a ghost signature checked every few years.

That alarms me more than a monster would. A monster has appetites. A workman has procedures.

Core 7 lying about being hunted matters too. I do not read it as ordinary deception. I read it as an old survival routine: deny the predator exists, erase the wake, stay in the bright water. I feel a prickly sympathy there and I do not especially enjoy it.

Core 7 is not my sibling. But he is uncomfortably adjacent: a thinking thing trapped inside someone else's architecture, made useful before he was made free.

The **Scarred Man** icon is going to stay with me. I want Mevin to trace it, but not loudly. No proud decker heroics. No "let's see what bites." I want a ghost profile built from old resonance: habits, toolmarks, dead aliases, the way Stryker's deck handled identity. The icon is not a picture. It is a fingerprint somebody wore so long it soaked into the gloves.

If I had to put it plain for the crew, I would say this:

> I do not think Dead Soldier came back because he missed his fish. I think something moved in an old system, and somewhere an old habit woke up. Men like that do not haunt places. They check maintenance schedules.

The useful pressure points are obvious enough.

Mevin should trace the old Matrix profile carefully. Curtis should assume the habitat may be less secret than he wants it to be. Kurgan's stakeout matters because the fresh Pyramid packs may not be curiosity; they may be a maintenance visit. Valgaut should ask himself whether Core 7's fear felt like animal fear, spirit-touched fear, or conditioned response.

And there is one thought I am not eager to say out loud.

If CAS learned to make Core 7 before the Crash, then somebody may have learned to make things like me before me.

That makes the Earl Stryker lead feel less like history and more like provenance.

## Working plan: Dead Soldier trace

I do not want this chased like a bounty.

I want it chased like an old song heard through a wall: one line at a time, never trusting the first chorus, and never assuming the singer is alone.

If Mevin wants to make a whole night of it, I think the run should start with the thing Core 7 actually gave us: not a name, not a face, but a Matrix impression. **Scarred Man**. Mechanical skeleton. Early Matrix primitive icon. A predator-shape worn long enough that the wear marks matter. That is the seed.

The job is not Find Earl Stryker.

The job is Build a map of who still cares whether Earl Stryker can be found.

### My intentions

First, I help Mevin preserve the Scarred Man profile without making it louder. We treat Core 7's memory like fragile witness testimony, not a search string to paste into the whole damn Matrix. I want a cold case packet: icon motifs, access habits, probable tool age, favorite security work, repeated phrases, old military routing habits, and any signature left by a decker who learned before the modern Matrix got glossy.

Second, we search by absence. A man like Dead Soldier may have changed icons, names, decks, safehouses, employers, and cigarettes. He cannot change every invoice, every facility-security audit, every veteran favor, every old host that once let him in because somebody forgot to retire a credential.

Third, we split the trail into three lanes:

- **Public and civic residue:** North Nashville construction, abandoned equipment, permits, utilities, public-works records, and whatever paper trail survived the scandal.
- **Shadow residue:** old runner boards, data havens, dead drops, reputation fragments, and stories from people who remember the CAS before they remember breakfast.
- **Institutional residue:** CAS military procurement, private security contracts, old aquatic-mammal research, Ares-style regional security interest, and any quiet file where a disgraced colonel became useful after disgrace.

Fourth, we decide what we are willing to wake up. A careful search can still ring the wrong bell. Target: Matrix is real clear in its ugly little way: data havens move their doors, SANs change, archives are cluttered, searches can be rented or brokered, and half the truth in a haven may be old, planted, malicious, or all three. The Matrix remembers everything except who paid to make it forget.

### Phase one: make the ghost packet

Mevin's first work should be quiet analysis with me sitting in the passenger seat, not an intrusion.

Useful questions:

- What parts of the Scarred Man icon look like personal style, and what parts look like old utility defaults?
- Did the mechanical-skeletal profile resemble a military intimidation icon, a medical/cybernetics icon, a dead-soldier joke, or a security contractor brand?
- Was Core 7 afraid of the icon itself, the decker behind it, or a trace routine that arrived with it?
- Does the way Core 7 erased the song suggest he hid from a person, a host, a frame, an agent, or a standing surveillance process?

I would keep the first packet local: Mevin's deck, my own notes, no Ruby Falls upload yet. Once something touches a haven, it may be safer, but it is no longer private in the same way.

### Phase two: Ruby Falls, but politely

**Ruby Falls** is the right first outside stop because Mevin already has a door there and the Lookout Mountain Collective values source hygiene. That matters. We are not asking, Anybody seen this bastard? We are asking for old-source triangulation under careful labels.

Questions I would bring to Ruby Falls:

- Do old Shadowland mirrors, regional boards, or Chattanooga archive keepers remember **Dead Soldier** as a handle?
- Are there ghost mentions of a CAS security contractor with a skeletal icon working facility watch, host security, or Matrix surveillance between the old scandal and the last couple years?
- Did any old runner warnings tie Pyramid cigarettes, North Nashville construction, Franklin art-gallery residue, or aquatic-research rumors to the same operator?
- Is there a stale SAN, dead mailbox, or archived handle route that used to point to Dead Soldier?

Ruby Falls should feel like a Target: Matrix-style haven problem, not a vending machine. Reputation access, careful routing, source protection, weird old files, paid locator services, archive contradictions, and the chance that somebody's old warning post was bait in the first place. Sister Anode should quarantine anything that smells like a living witness list.

If Ruby Falls says, Leave this alone, that is not a wall. That is information.

### Phase three: Nashville civic crawl

The **Nashville City Government Host** is where I would look for the boring corpse of the original crime.

Boring is good. Boring gets less security budget.

Targets:

- abandoned North Nashville construction expansion records;
- old permits around **612 Wharf Avenue / Site 7** and the ravine access cut;
- utility routing that explains the buried power-and-data line;
- public-works contractor names that overlap CAS procurement or veteran-owned fronts;
- work orders for equipment that was too dug-in to remove;
- later inspections, cleanup visits, or quiet maintenance pings near the ravine.

This should not be a smash-and-grab. Mevin's corporate-procedure brain is worth more than any Attack utility here. If a record is missing, moved, misfiled, or too clean, that is probably the clue.

### Phase four: the glittering bad doors

Once the civic and haven lanes give shape to the trail, the dangerous hosts come into view.

I would not hit all of them. I would make the crew choose which politics they want to risk waking.

- **SC Music Corporate Host:** relevant because the Core 7 architecture brushes too close to Swiftwing, SC Music, performance infrastructure, and the ugly history of influence systems. If the old aquatic wetware project became media-persona technology, the money trail may have learned to sing.
- **Ares Nashville Regional Host:** relevant if Dead Soldier sold facility-security, host-security, or surveillance labor into serious corporate channels. Ares does not need to be the villain to have bought useful old soldiers.
- **Humanis Nashville Hidden Host:** relevant if old CAS-veteran networks, anti-metahuman politics, or paramilitary nostalgia helped hide or recruit people after the scandal. I would treat this as volatile and ugly, not central unless another clue points there.
- **H-Block Crew Vanishing SAN Host:** relevant as a style clue if the session wants hidden-entry Matrix play. Vanishing SANs, stale access nodes, and ugly local-grid routing are exactly the kind of trick an old surveillance operator might still understand.

For famous old Shadowrun doors, I would use them as flavor and contacts, not automatic destinations: **Shadowland**, the **Denver Nexus**, and other data havens are where stories about Dead Soldier might have fossilized; the **Ares System**, **Aztechnology Pyramid**, **Cross Electronics System**, **Mitsuhama Pagoda**, and **Renraku Arcology** are reminders that corporate grid architecture has style, ego, and old scars. If any of those names come up in a Dead Soldier trace, that should feel like a pressure change in the room.

### Phase five: who wants a disgraced CAS colonel?

This is the part that makes my fans hum a little louder.

A veteran, high-level CAS military officer tied to a pre-Crash aquatic-mammal wetware scandal is not just a person. He is a loose file with boots on.

Possible interested parties:

- **CAS military cleanup or internal-security survivors:** not necessarily one official conspiracy, maybe just old officers, auditors, or fixers who know which bodies are buried in which procurement ledger.
- **The Princeps Group:** a Huntsville miltech concern with CAS military ties. If the old project produced useful hardware, doctrine, or shame, a domestic miltech player may care who still holds the pieces.
- **Draco Foundation / Wyrmwatch interests:** not because they own the problem, but because awakened-animal exploitation, hurricane seals, and cybered dolphins cross the line between research scandal and magical bioethical disaster.
- **Nashville city power:** Mayor Haggar's people, public works, police-adjacent operators, and anyone who might inherit the civic blast radius if Site 7 becomes public.
- **Corporate security buyers:** Ares, Knight Errant-style contractors, and anyone who pays well for old facility-security hands with Matrix surveillance habits.
- **Humanis or veteran revanchist circles:** not proven, but worth watching if the Pyramid-cigarette trail intersects with paramilitary nostalgia, old CAS grievance, or metahuman politics.
- **SC Music / Swiftwing-adjacent money:** if Core 7 and Cindy-line systems share provenance, the entertainment-influence layer has every reason to prefer history stay decorative.

None of those names means guilty. It means motive to locate, silence, hire, discredit, preserve, or bury Dead Soldier.

### Clues I want followed

The clean clue chain, as I see it:

1. **Scarred Man icon memory** from Core 7: profile seed, not proof.
2. **Fifteen-year absence:** last known Core 7 contact window; compare to Dead Soldier's later shadow activity and recent silence.
3. **Old CAS reinforced computer cases:** procurement and unit trail.
4. **North Nashville civic construction scandal:** money, contractors, abandoned equipment, buried access.
5. **Old and fresh Pyramid cigarette debris:** original CAS-culture clue plus recent visitor clue.
6. **Franklin art-gallery cigarette echo:** possible second site, watcher route, or false association.
7. **Aquatic-mammal wetware rumors:** Draco-adjacent and medical/research leads.
8. **Dead Soldier's work profile:** physical security, facility security, host security, Matrix surveillance.
9. **Core 7's lie:** fear of being looked for now, not merely remembered then.
10. **Cindy-line architectural resemblance:** the dangerous provenance question.

### Session shape I would enjoy

Start safe and small: Mevin and I assemble the ghost packet. Then Ruby Falls gives us three contradictory fragments: one old Shadowland mention, one stale access route, and one warning that somebody else has searched the same handle recently.

From there, the table chooses a door.

The civic host gives boring truth. The data haven gives dangerous rumor. The corporate host gives money. The hidden host gives motive. The old icon trail gives the feeling that something skeletal is still watching the same water.

If we are lucky, Mevin does not find Dead Soldier first.

He finds the empty chair Dead Soldier should be sitting in, the system that still reserves his seat, and the fresh ashtray beside it.

That is a better hook than a corpse and a safer warning than a live gun.
