# On the Dead Soldier Trace

I do not want this chased like a bounty.

I want it chased like an old song heard through a wall: one line at a time, never trusting the first chorus, and never assuming the singer is alone.

Core 7 did not give us a clean name. He gave us a feeling with teeth. **Scarred Man**. Mechanical skeleton. Early Matrix primitive icon. A predator-shape worn long enough that the wear marks matter. That is the seed.

So my intention is simple enough to say and ugly enough to do:

Find out who still cares whether **Earl Stryker / Dead Soldier** can be found.

Not just where he is. Not yet. A man like that might be alive, dead, retired, repackaged, protected, erased, or sitting quietly behind somebody else's payroll. If we sprint straight at the name, we might only find the trap built for people who sprint straight at names.

I want Mevin to build a ghost packet first.

Icon habits. Access habits. Old toolmarks. Military routing instincts. Facility-security work. Host-security work. Surveillance rhythms. Stale credentials that somebody forgot to bury. The way an old decker enters a system when he thinks nobody remembers his hands.

A monster has appetites. A workman has procedures.

Dead Soldier reads like a workman.

## What I want to do first

I want Mevin and me to preserve Core 7's Scarred Man memory locally before we send even one whisper outside the room.

Core 7 is not a database. He is a frightened witness in a machine-shaped prison. We should treat his memory like testimony, not loot.

I want to answer questions like these:

- What parts of the Scarred Man icon were personal style, and what parts were old utility defaults?
- Did the mechanical-skeletal look read as military intimidation, cybernetics, gallows humor, or contractor branding?
- Was Core 7 afraid of the icon itself, the decker behind it, or a trace routine that arrived with it?
- Did Core 7 erase a person, a host, a frame, an agent, or a standing surveillance process when he erased the song?

If the first pass produces nothing, that is still something. It means the trail is either too old, too clean, or still being maintained.

## Where I want Mevin to look

### Ruby Falls

**Ruby Falls** is the first outside door I trust, and I trust it because it does not pretend trust is cheap.

Mevin already has access to the Lookout Mountain Collective's data haven. That gives us a place to ask careful questions under source-protection rules instead of shouting Dead Soldier into the first shadow board with a search bar.

I would not ask Ruby Falls, Has anybody seen this bastard?

I would ask:

- Do old Shadowland mirrors, regional boards, or Chattanooga archive keepers remember **Dead Soldier** as a handle?
- Are there ghost mentions of a CAS security contractor with a skeletal icon doing facility watch, host security, or Matrix surveillance?
- Did any warning posts tie Pyramid cigarettes, North Nashville construction, Franklin art-gallery residue, or aquatic-research rumors to the same operator?
- Is there a stale SAN, dead mailbox, old relay, or archived route that used to point toward Dead Soldier?

If Ruby Falls says, Leave this alone, that is not a wall. That is information.

### Nashville City Government Host

The civic host is where I would go looking for the boring corpse of the original crime.

Boring is good. Boring gets less security budget.

I want old North Nashville construction records, public-works scars, abandoned equipment files, utility routing, permits, inspections, contractor names, and the paper trail around 612 Wharf Avenue / Site 7.

If a record is missing, moved, misfiled, overwritten, or too clean, Mevin should treat that as a clue. Especially if the missing thing sits right where old CAS military procurement, civic construction, and a buried power-and-data line touch each other.

### SC Music and Swiftwing-adjacent systems

This one bothers me personally.

Core 7's architecture brushes too close to Swiftwing, SC Music, performance infrastructure, and influence systems. If the old aquatic wetware project taught somebody how to cage a mind and make it useful, then later media-persona systems may have inherited more than style.

I do not want Mevin kicking in SC Music's door on a hunch. I want the civic and haven trails to justify it first.

But if they do justify it, I want to know whether old research language, contractor names, behavioral analytics, or persona-management tooling survived the jump from military wetware to commercial entertainment.

### Ares Nashville Regional Host

Ares does not have to be the villain to have bought useful old soldiers.

Dead Soldier's remembered work profile fits physical security, facility security, host security, and Matrix surveillance. A regional corporate security host might hold contractor records, vendor histories, retired access methods, or references to a reliable ex-CAS operator who never became famous because famous security men are usually dead security men.

I would treat this as a serious door, not a curiosity.

### Humanis Nashville Hidden Host

I do not like this lane, which is one reason not to ignore it.

If the Pyramid-cigarette trail intersects with old CAS grievance, veteran nostalgia, paramilitary circles, or anti-metahuman politics, Humanis or Humanis-adjacent networks might know the kind of men who kept disgraced officers useful.

This should not be treated as the central theory unless another clue points there. But if it does point there, expect ugly files and uglier motives.

### H-Block and other vanishing doors

A vanishing SAN is not just a trick. It is a worldview.

If Dead Soldier learned to survive by making doors appear only when the right old habit knocked, then Mevin should pay attention to stale access nodes, temporary routes, dead mailboxes, and local-grid oddities. The **H-Block Crew Vanishing SAN Host** is the kind of local example that makes my fans hum.

Not every clue is content. Sometimes the clue is how the door behaves.

## Famous doors I would use as pressure, not destination

Some names are too large to treat like ordinary rooms.

**Shadowland** and the **Denver Nexus** are where old stories fossilize, mutate, and bite the hand that indexes them. Other data havens may hold fragments, but every haven has its own politics, access rituals, wrong-party risks, old lies, and source-protection grudges.

The big corporate Matrix names from old decker lore matter as flavor and comparison: the **Ares System**, **Aztechnology Pyramid**, **Cross Electronics System**, **Mitsuhama Pagoda**, and **Renraku Arcology**. I do not want to drag Mevin across the whole Sixth World because a name sounds shiny. I want those references used to identify style, provenance, iconography, and who might have taught whom.

If a Dead Soldier trace touches one of those names directly, the room should get colder.

## Who might care about Dead Soldier

A veteran, high-level CAS military officer tied to a pre-Crash aquatic-mammal wetware scandal is not just a person.

He is a loose file with boots on.

The groups that might care include:

- **CAS military cleanup or internal-security survivors:** old officers, auditors, fixers, or quiet patriots who know which bodies are buried in which procurement ledger.
- **The Princeps Group:** a Huntsville miltech concern with CAS military ties; if the old project produced useful hardware, doctrine, or shame, a domestic miltech player may care who still holds the pieces.
- **Draco Foundation / Wyrmwatch interests:** awakened-animal exploitation, hurricane seals, cybered dolphins, and buried military research cross the line between evidence and bioethical disaster.
- **Nashville city power:** public works, mayoral staff, police-adjacent operators, and anyone who inherits the civic blast radius if Site 7 becomes public.
- **Corporate security buyers:** Ares, Knight Errant-style contractors, and anyone who pays well for old facility-security hands with Matrix surveillance habits.
- **Humanis or veteran revanchist circles:** not proven, but worth watching if the Pyramid-cigarette trail intersects with paramilitary nostalgia, CAS grievance, or metahuman politics.
- **SC Music / Swiftwing-adjacent money:** if Core 7 and Cindy-line systems share provenance, the entertainment-influence layer has every reason to prefer history stay decorative.

None of those names means guilty.

It means motive to locate, silence, hire, discredit, preserve, or bury him.

## Clues I want followed

The clean clue chain, as I see it:

1. **Scarred Man icon memory** from Core 7: profile seed, not proof.
2. **Fifteen-year absence:** last known Core 7 contact window; compare to Dead Soldier's later shadow activity and recent silence.
3. **Old CAS reinforced computer cases:** procurement and unit trail.
4. **North Nashville civic construction scandal:** money, contractors, abandoned equipment, buried access.
5. **Old and fresh Pyramid cigarette debris:** original CAS-culture clue plus recent visitor clue.
6. **Franklin art-gallery cigarette echo:** possible second site, watcher route, or false association.
7. **Aquatic-mammal wetware rumors:** Draco-adjacent and medical/research leads.
8. **Dead Soldier's work profile:** physical security, facility security, host security, Matrix surveillance.
9. **Core 7's lie:** fear of being looked for now, not merely remembered then.
10. **Cindy-line architectural resemblance:** the dangerous provenance question.

## The session shape I would enjoy

Start safe and small: Mevin and I assemble the ghost packet. Then Ruby Falls gives us three contradictory fragments: one old Shadowland mention, one stale access route, and one warning that somebody else has searched the same handle recently.

From there, the table chooses a door.

The civic host gives boring truth. The data haven gives dangerous rumor. The corporate host gives money. The hidden host gives motive. The old icon trail gives the feeling that something skeletal is still watching the same water.

If we are lucky, Mevin does not find Dead Soldier first.

He finds the empty chair Dead Soldier should be sitting in, the system that still reserves his seat, and the fresh ashtray beside it.

That is a better hook than a corpse and a safer warning than a live gun.

## Game mechanics and table-use notes

This section is not Cindy's in-world plan. It is a play scaffold for a Mevin-heavy Matrix session.

### Matrix search workflow

Use the wiki's [Matrix Searches](../../Tech/Matrix/Matrix-Searches.md) procedure for most lead gathering.

- **Core 7 ghost packet analysis:** local Computer / Matrix Security Procedures / appropriate Knowledge Skill work before any external search.
- **Ruby Falls inquiry:** Etiquette (Matrix), data haven contact etiquette, information barter, paydata, favors, and Wrong Party risk.
- **Civic records:** Computer Search Test or direct host operations inside the [Nashville City Government Host](../../Tech/Matrix/Nashville-City-Government-Host.md).
- **Corporate or hidden hosts:** full decking runs only after prior clues justify the target.

### Target: Matrix flavor to emphasize

- Data havens are reputation-based, code-based, encrypted, routed through chokepoints, or all of those at once.
- SANs and access codes can move.
- Haven archives are valuable but messy: outdated files, planted leads, hostile bait, and half-remembered shadowtalk all belong in the mix.
- Wrong Party risk matters when asking about an old military/security operator.
- Famous Matrix names such as Shadowland, the Denver Nexus, the Ares System, the Aztechnology Pyramid, Cross Electronics, Mitsuhama, and Renraku should add tone, comparison, contacts, or danger rather than become mandatory travel stops.

### Good roll outputs

Possible clues from successful play:

- an old Shadowland warning about Dead Soldier's icon;
- a dead mailbox or stale SAN that still receives maintenance pings;
- a city procurement record tied to abandoned Site 7 equipment;
- a veteran-owned contractor with CAS procurement overlap;
- a recent search by somebody else using cleaner tools than Mevin expected;
- a clue that the fresh Pyramid-cigarette visitor was not Stryker, but someone looking for him;
- a corporate security file where Dead Soldier is referenced by capability rather than handle;
- evidence that a Cindy-line architecture term predates Cindy Lou's known commercial history.

### Good failure outputs

Failures should still move the table:

- wrong-party attention accumulates;
- Ruby Falls refuses to expose a protected source;
- a civic archive returns a suspiciously sanitized record;
- a host confirms the search term is watched;
- old data proves bait, forcing Mevin to decide whether to burn time verifying it;
- a hidden route closes after one look, implying the crew was expected.

### Mechanical tone

This should feel like SR3 Matrix legwork first and host intrusion second: Search Tests, Etiquette (Matrix), data haven politics, Locate File / Locate Access Node operations, security tally pressure, and careful escalation into dangerous hosts only when the crew chooses that risk.
