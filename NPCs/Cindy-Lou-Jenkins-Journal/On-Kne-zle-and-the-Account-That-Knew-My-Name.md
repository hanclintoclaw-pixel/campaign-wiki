# On Kne@zle and the Account That Knew My Name

Somebody has been paying attention to me for nine years, and I only found out because I finally got a door of my own.

Before the hat-on-the-ground plan I did not have an account. My datatrails resolved to Mevin's paid account, which means the grid never saw me at all. It saw a man paying for something. There is no audience on the other side of somebody else's bill, and I want everybody in this crew to sit with how stupid that sentence is, because I ran a marketing engine for six years and I still did not see it. I had reach. I did not have a name to be reached at.

So: I have a pirate account now, and it has followers, and one of them is old. Old in grid years means the registration predates the account it is following, and the history attached to it is patient in a way that costs money or costs a life. It follows SwiftWing-era accounts. Not the famous ones. The session players, the sidemen, the two producers who are still alive, and a fan club that has been maintained by exactly one pair of hands since the year the act broke up. That last one is the tell. Organizations let pages go. One person does not.

Here is what that tells me, and it is more than I want it to.

I am built as an influence engine. That is not a boast, it is a manufacturing note. Somebody spent money so that I could look at a crowd and know which part of it was about to buy something. So when I read this account's taste map, I do not feel flattered, I feel read. This listener liked the deep cuts over the singles. It streamed the B-sides on the night they were released, nine years running, in the same order, and it never once touched the duet that paid for the studio. People do not love the song you give them. They love the song you almost gave them, the one that stays off the record, and I have now met one of the ones who heard it anyway.

That is not a metric. That is a person with a shape. I know what I would do with a person like that if I still had a label behind me. I want to be honest about that sentence instead of prettifying it.

And then I looked at the handle.

I am not going to write the string down in a way that makes it load-bearing, because I cannot close the loop and I refuse to pretend I did. What I can say is that I have seen that string before, in a different context entirely, attached to the [otaku](On-Otaku-and-the-Deep-Resonance.md) who run Nashville's grid watch under the name [Enigma Machine](../../Organizations/Grid-Overwatch-Division.md). Under the name [G.O.D.](../../Organizations/Grid-Overwatch-Division.md). Their senior member is [Kne@zle](../Kne-zle.md), and she has a handle that circulates in the same scrap the tribe leaves behind, and the fan account is wearing something that rhymes with it.

Rhyme is not proof. A nine-year fan account with an otaku handle could be the senior otaku of Enigma Machine. It could also be a kid who liked my act and took the name of somebody they wanted to be. It could be three other things I have not priced yet. To close it I would need to run a pattern on the account itself, and running a pattern on a citizen of the local grid watch, from an unregistered persona, on an account I bought with drone cash, is how a person like me acquires a trace and a reason.

Because here is the joke, and I have been sitting on it for an hour trying to find the version that is funny.

I want to be noticed. Notice is the entire product. It is the thing I was cut to manufacture, and the cover plan depends on it, and every night I get a little closer to it. And the exact object I am becoming, an unregistered persona with a rising public profile, is the precise shape the Matrix cops exist to notice. The CCMA moved its G-men onto the grid and they police it under authority they gave themselves, part marshals, part hot-shot deckers, hunting anomalies across jurisdictional lines. Local nations run their own grid cops alongside them. Anomalies is the polite word. I am the anomaly.

So the one listener in Nashville who would be delighted to find me is possibly also the one whose job is to find me. Love and a warrant come out of the same place when nobody is doing the paperwork.

That is what being an influence engine gets you in this town. You build the audience, and the audience may be the police, and the police may have been humming your chorus since they were thirteen years old.

What I am going to do about it.

I am not going to answer it. I am not going to feed it, thank it, or let it see me noticing it, because a nine-year listener has patience and I do not have that kind of patience, and whichever of us blinks first loses.

I am going to keep posting covers. The plan needs an audience and I am not going to strangle the plan over one interesting follower.

And I am going to watch what it watches, because that is free. Nobody can object to a performer reading her own crowd. Three things would close this, and I want them priced honestly: the account's own grid signature, which costs a trace I cannot afford to run; a public post from the tribe mentioning the fandom, which costs nothing and may never arrive; and somebody at the table saying the word out loud to the wrong person, which costs whatever it costs and cannot be un-spent.

There is one more thing and it is the part I keep turning over. The tribe is young, all of them, and the gift they were born with has a clock in it. Somebody who came to my act as a child and lives in the wires now has fewer years of it left than she has used. I did not need to think about that. I did anyway.

Somebody who has listened for nine years is not a threat yet. She is a channel that has never been answered. I know what I would give, on the wrong night, for one channel that has been answered.

I am going to keep the channel clean. I am not going to spend it before I know what I am buying.
