# Curtis Backpack Arms Build

Curtis is beginning a staged Drone Shift project to build a wearable retractable backpack rig with six compact mechanical arms: three per side, spidered off a central backplate, folding down into a small backpack profile, and carrying swappable tool or light-combat end effectors.

The attached concept shows a compact metal spine/backpack with a shoulder-and-waist harness, six articulated arms, quick-change tool tips, and a folded carry profile. The campaign version should be garage-built, wearable for long periods, light enough to carry, strong enough for useful tool work, and safe enough not to wrench Curtis's spine sideways the first time an arm catches a doorframe.

## Design Constraints

- **Wearable first:** load transfers through shoulder straps, a rigid backplate, and a waist/hip belt rather than hanging entirely from Curtis's shoulders.
- **Light but tough:** use aircraft aluminum, titanium alloy samples, drone-arm salvaged linkages, high-strength pivots, and replaceable sacrificial bushings rather than full steel limbs.
- **Retractable:** each arm must fold or nest into a locked backpack profile before any combat/tool rating is considered.
- **Tool before weapon:** first working functions are clamps, probes, drivers, cutters, and stabilizers. Blades or combat striking need GM approval after safety testing.
- **No free cyberware:** this is external wearable equipment, not extra natural limbs, extra cyberlimbs, or automatic extra attacks.
- **GM acceptance gate:** no permanent equipment rating, combat modifier, drone-dashboard state, or sheet benefit applies until the final acceptance report is approved.

## Drone Shift Build Sequence

Daily Drone Shift work orders should pull from this sequence until the build is complete or the GM changes priority.

1. **Load-path sketch and material coupons** - define the harness load path, choose first lightweight metal samples, and log the folded envelope.
2. **Backplate and hip-belt skeleton** - fabricate the rigid spine plate and hip load-transfer frame.
3. **Folded-profile dummy pack** - make a nonpowered cardboard/wood/aluminum mockup proving the arms can nest into a wearable backpack outline.
4. **Arm segment pattern** - cut and drill one repeatable three-segment arm pattern with service holes and bushing seats.
5. **Root joint cluster** - build the central shoulder/root joint hub for one side of the pack.
6. **Retraction rails and lock detents** - add rails, stops, and mechanical locks so folded arms stay folded and deployed arms stay braced.
7. **Actuator test mule** - choose a first actuation style for one arm: electric micro-servos, cable drive, or hydraulic/pneumatic assist.
8. **Power and control trunk** - route battery, fusing, manual cutoff, and low-level controls through the backplate.
9. **Quick-change wrist sockets** - fabricate swappable tool sockets with at least one clamp/probe/driver test set.
10. **Single-arm lift and tool test** - prove one arm can hold position, use a tool, and fail safely under load.
11. **Three-arm side assembly** - build one complete side with three arms and folded/deployed synchronization.
12. **Mirror-side replication** - duplicate the opposite side and correct asymmetry.
13. **Wear test and snag test** - walk, crouch, climb into Grandpa, and move through doorways without dangerous snags or balance problems.
14. **Final acceptance and GM review** - write the permanent-gear proposal: final weight, limitations, maintenance needs, safe end effectors, and any requested rules effect.
