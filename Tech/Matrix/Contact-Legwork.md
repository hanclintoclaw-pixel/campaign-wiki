# Contact Legwork

This workflow turns SR3 contact rules into a table procedure for asking NPC contacts for information, introductions, services, or gear. It is a play aid, not a replacement for the books; the GM still decides what a contact could plausibly know, whether they are available, what they want, and how loudly the inquiry echoes through the shadows.

## Pick the legwork job

Use the narrowest path that fits the ask.

1. **Ask a known contact directly:** the runner calls or meets an existing contact and asks for information or help.
2. **Ask the contact to check around:** the contact does not know immediately, but can listen for news and report back.
3. **Ask for an introduction:** the runner goes through a Friend of a Friend (FOF) because the original contact is not the right person.
4. **Find goods or services:** the runner uses contacts, fixers, or FOFs to locate gear, people, safehouses, documents, permits, or specialized services.
5. **Use a special contact:** Shadowland, a club/organization member, an international contact, a fixer, or another broad access point.

## Step 1: Choose the right contact

The first SR3 filter is not a roll. Ask whether the contact is appropriate.

- A Lone Star contact may know precinct rumors, police procedures, arrests, local warrants, or security chatter.
- A corporate contact may know corporate routines, guard schedules, office politics, internal projects, or executive gossip.
- A talismonger may know magical rumors, formulae, telesma, awakened contacts, or occult customers.
- A fixer may know many people, but usually expects nuyen or a future favor.
- A bartender, ganger, street doc, decker, reporter, or Johnson each points toward a different information ecosystem.

If the contact is wrong for the question, the useful result may be an introduction, a rumor, or a dead end rather than the answer.

## Step 2: Immediate information from a contact

Use this when the runner asks a known contact a direct question.

1. **Set the social skill.** Use the appropriate Etiquette specialization, usually Street, Corporate, Matrix, Tribal, Magical, Mercenary, or another table-approved fit.
2. **Start at TN 4.** SR3 gives Etiquette (Street or Corporate) TN 4 as the typical legwork test to find out what a contact knows and is willing to spill.
3. **Apply contact level dice.**
   - Level 1: no bonus dice.
   - Level 2: +1 die to Etiquette Tests made to acquire information from that contact.
   - Level 3: +2 dice to Etiquette Tests made to acquire information from that contact.
4. **Roll Etiquette.** More successes reveal more of the available success table or more detail at the GM's discretion. More than one success includes all lower-success information.
5. **Roleplay the exchange.** SR3 explicitly frames contacts as people, not vending machines. The meeting, attitude, price, favors, and risk should fit the contact.

## Step 3: Separate knowing from talking

Shadowrun Companion adds a useful two-part distinction: what the contact knows and what the contact is willing to reveal.

### Does the contact know?

The GM may roll the contact's Etiquette or Intelligence, whichever fits, using the Contact Knowledge Table. One success usually means the contact knows that level of information and lower levels.

- TN 2: general information.
- TN 4: more details.
- TN 6: even more details.
- TN 8-11: specific names, places, and valuable specifics.
- TN 12-13: information not yet on the streets.
- TN 14+: information beyond the realm of the streets.

Useful modifiers from the table:

- contact's specialty: -2 TN.
- contact is a fixer or Mr. Johnson: -3 TN.

If this test fails, the contact may know nothing, may only have a rumor, or may accidentally/maliciously pass bad information.

### Will the contact talk?

The usual simple method is the runner's Etiquette Test. Companion suggests default TN 4, modified by the subject's sensitivity and the contact's situation. A snitch may talk about a local gang at TN 4, while questions about a Mafia boss could be TN 12.

For more contested scenes, the GM may use a Negotiation Success Contest between the character and contact. The Contact Success Contest modifiers can apply to bargaining or other negotiation, including factors such as suspicion, whether the requested outcome benefits or harms the contact, contact level, debts, hostility, cultural gaps, language barriers, and extra nuyen.

## Step 4: Ask the contact to check around

Use this when the contact does not know immediately but can listen for news.

1. The GM makes an appropriate Etiquette Test for the contact.
2. Apply +2 TN to the contact's test.
3. Use the relevant legwork/success-table target number if one exists.
4. If the contact gets successes, the contact reports back in 2D6 hours or at a GM-set time.
5. Fee is determined like normal contact information.

This is a good GM tool for moving important information to the runners without making the contact instantly omniscient.

## Step 5: Calculate contact fees

SR3 offers a baseline formula for information fees:

**Contact's Charisma x Contact's Intelligence x 50, divided by Etiquette successes = base fee in nuyen.**

Then adjust for the fiction.

- A close friend may waive or reduce the fee.
- A fixer may demand cash or a future favor.
- A sensitive ask may cost more.
- Standard Negotiation procedures can adjust final payment.
- A contact may prefer barter, protection, a job, paydata, introductions, or a marker instead of nuyen.

## Step 6: Use Friends of a Friend

Use FOFs when the original contact is not the right person, but knows someone who might be.

### Who does a contact know?

Shadowrun Companion says most contacts begin with one Level 1, one Level 2, and one Level 3 FOF. Fixers and Mr. Johnsons know two contacts at each level because their job depends on networks.

### FOF modifiers

When dealing with a FOF, make the appropriate test and apply the FOF level modifier:

- Level 1 FOF: +6 TN, cost/time multiplier x3, Wrong Party modifier -2.
- Level 2 FOF: +4 TN, cost/time multiplier x2, Wrong Party modifier -1.
- Level 3 FOF: +2 TN, cost/time multiplier x1, no listed Wrong Party modifier.
- Each FOF after the first: +2 TN, cost/time multiplier x2, Wrong Party modifier -3.
- Asking around: use the GM's text guidance; it is broader, slower, and riskier than a clean direct ask.

Any bonus dice from the primary contact still apply when interacting with that contact's FOFs.

## Step 7: Waiting for information or goods

For a FOF locating information or equipment:

1. If the item has Availability, use its Availability time as normal.
2. If the request has no Availability, roll 2D6 days as the base waiting period.
3. Multiply by the FOF cost/time multiplier.
4. Reduce the waiting period by 1 day for each Etiquette Test success the character spends on speed.
5. Reduce the waiting period by 1 day for each additional 10 percent of the contact fee paid.

For gear, after the object is located, SR3 Street Gear rules still apply: street price starts from base cost x Street Index, then Negotiation can move price by 5 percent per net success. If a deal falls through because the buyer refuses the price, that contact may be harder to use next time.

## Step 8: Check Wrong Party risk

Any time runners discuss biz with a contact or FOF, someone else might hear.

1. The GM rolls a Wrong Party Test every time a contact or FOF is used.
2. Dice equal the number of individuals involved in the inquiry.
3. Base TN is 6.
4. The GM can raise TN for careful/paranoid play or lower it for careless play.
5. FOF Wrong Party modifiers lower the TN, making leaks more likely.
6. Successes are cumulative across the whole line of inquiry.

Wrong Party consequence bands from Companion:

- 1-4 successes: word is on the street; enemies and rivals may start listening.
- 5-8 successes: the street knows enough to form theories; targets and competitors may react.
- 9-12 successes: opposition may know enough to interfere seriously.
- 13+ successes: the line of inquiry is badly blown; expect traps, death squads, Matrix warfare, private investigators, or harassment campaigns as fits the campaign.

The GM may also respond with disinformation rather than immediate violence.

## Step 9: Special contacts

### Fixers

Fixers are broad network brokers. They can find gear, people, work, services, and information, but rarely work for free. A specialist fixer should have secondary contacts that match the specialty.

### Clubs and organizations

A contact inside a club, policlub, corp department, union, gang, magical group, or similar organization may create a chain of FOFs. Companion suggests rolling 2D6 / 2, rounded up, for how many people the character must wade through before reaching a knowledgeable FOF.

### International contacts

International FOF chains use the same FOF logic, with special modifiers for things like international groups, law enforcement, customs/border patrol, enemies looking for the character, travel time, and smuggling class.

### Shadowland

Shadowland is a special contact, usually Level 2, available to characters with Computer 1+ at character creation. It is not private: once information is on Shadowland, other people can find it too. Searches take time and may count as FOF-style interactions. For Matrix-specific Shadowland searching, use [Matrix Searches](Matrix-Searches.md).

## Quick table procedure

Before rolling, answer these in order:

1. What exactly are you asking for?
2. Which contact is being used, and why are they appropriate?
3. Are you asking directly, asking them to check around, or asking for an introduction?
4. What Etiquette specialization applies?
5. What is the TN: default 4, Contact Knowledge Table, Availability, FOF modifier, or GM-adjusted sensitivity TN?
6. What contact level bonus dice apply?
7. What does the contact want: nuyen, favor, barter, paydata, silence, or protection?
8. How long will it take?
9. How many people are now involved for Wrong Party risk?
10. What result can the player report to the GM?

## GM-facing result format

Use this compact report after a legwork attempt:

```text
Contact Legwork Report
Ask: [specific question/request]
Contact: [name/type/level]
Route: direct / check around / FOF / fixer / Shadowland / organization
Roll: [skill] vs TN [number], [successes] successes
Payment/Favor: [nuyen, favor, barter, none, pending]
Wait: [immediate, 2D6 hours, Availability time, 2D6 days x FOF multiplier, GM-set]
Result: [what the contact knows, lead gained, no answer, bad lead, goods located]
Wrong Party: [dice/TN if known, successes if GM reveals them]
Next lead: [who/where/what to follow]
```
