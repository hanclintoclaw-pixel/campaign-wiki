# Swiftwing Records

## Canon status (GM ruling, New Cindy GM Interaction Thread, 2026-11-10)

No printed SR3 equivalent: **zero** matches across the 19-book local SR3 corpus. **Table-created** for this campaign - not a re-skin of a printed organization, and printed canon makes no claim about it.


## Overview

The record label that **bought** the virtual pop act **Cindy Lou Jenkins** and used her as an audience-analysis and market-shaping instrument. Swiftwing is the origin point of the campaign's long-running music / AI / influence-engine thread and the earliest formal setup of the Cindy rescue-investigation arc.

*Older records here named her "Cindy Liu Thompson"; that is a record error - see [Name authority](../NPCs/Cindy-Lou-Jenkins.md#name-authority-gm-ruling-2026-11-10).*

## What the crew knows

- Swiftwing **bought** Cindy rather than building her (GM ruling, 2026-11-10). The label owned and operated the platform; it is **not** the source of the underlying architecture, which is a cost profile a record label does not fit.
- Swiftwing ran Cindy as a **Matrix virtual pop star** - high-resolution icon, online-only tracks, e-merch - and **placed and defended the PR line that she was a real person** (GM ruling, 2026-11-10).
- Swiftwing **paid** to use her for deep audience analysis and market shaping. That was the product; the entertainment was the delivery vehicle.
- **The concept was not the product; the campaign was** (GM ruling, 2026-11-16). Cheap generated synth tracks were already a commodity at the bottom of the market, and a five-nuyen track is a product - it claims nothing about being alive. What Swiftwing backed was a **Faux Persona**: a persona held in circulation by a sustained, well-funded apparatus until the public stops asking whether it is real. See [What she was actually built for](../NPCs/Cindy-Lou-Jenkins.md).
- **The load-bearing premise was shyness** (GM ruling, 2026-11-16). The act never had to convince anyone that a singer with no body existed. It had to convince them that a real, live Cindy Lou Jenkins existed and simply declined to appear in public - so the one thing the persona could never deliver was sold as temperament.
- **Her two highest skills are the crowd skills** - Crowd Behavior Analysis **8** and Crowd Behavior Shaping **8** (GM grant, 2026-11-16) against Perform 6. Performing was the delivery mechanism; reading and moving an audience was the purchased capability. [Stat block](../NPCs/Cindy-Lou-Jenkins-Tooling/Cindys-In-Character-Stats.md).
- **Ale 8 endorsement:** Swiftwing's commercial goal included songs intentionally built to make listeners more likely to buy and drink Ale 8, for which Swiftwing was paid (GM ruling, 2026-11-10). The crew's observation of compulsive product promotion was this feature, not a malfunction.
- **The endorsement book was wider than beverages** (GM ruling, 2026-11-16). Cindy also recorded jingles for **SoyCorp Meatlets** and the **Augmented Beef and Bacon Social Club**, plus various unnamed local products. Every one of those placements ran as a viral marketing campaign and a subliminal influence operation at the same time. [Back catalog](../NPCs/Cindy-Lou-Jenkins-Back-Catalog.md#jingles-and-paid-influence-work-gm-ruling-2026-11-16).
- **What the label held** (GM ruling, 2026-11-16). Swiftwing owns the masters and the technical rights, and also **her name and her image**, which is why Cindy has to impersonate herself to e-busk. [Who owns what](../NPCs/Cindy-Lou-Jenkins-Back-Catalog.md#who-owns-what-gm-ruling-2026-11-16).
- The press line of exclusive underground shows - which any given fan was simply never lucky enough to have attended - was Swiftwing marketing architecture, not Cindy's performance history.
- The original job framing treated Cindy's physical host/server platform as the recoverable "body" in question.
- Swiftwing sits near the origin point of the campaign's long-running music / AI / influence-engine thread.
- By **2026-05-28**, session reasoning suggested Swiftwing may have been working from architecture, code, or commercially adapted design lineage traceable back toward **Core 7 / Site 7** rather than inventing Cindy's foundation in isolation.

## The audience machine (GM-approved integration, 2026-11-16)

Written up from Cindy's scan of the media landscape, approved for the wiki by the GM on 2026-11-16. This is the operational shape of what the label ran - not a new ruling about what Cindy is.

- Swiftwing operated a continuous pipeline: acquire attention, program appearances and rotation, promote the act, distribute channel-specific material, measure response, and monetize the resulting attachment. Each rung supplied the next; the audience response also informed what returned to circulation.
- Fandom made that pipeline durable. Attention converted to revenue, revenue supported continued contact and loyalty, and loyalty became leverage over what got made next. The subscriber relationship and the fan club outlasted a single sale; keeping people reachable mattered beyond any particular purchase.
- Paid endorsements occupied one rung of the monetization ladder alongside recordings, exclusives, and merchandise. Ale 8 demonstrates that rung. It does not account for the whole business or reduce Cindy's career to one beverage placement.
- Programming and distribution worked together: a short version could acquire listeners while host-only material gave existing fans a reason to return. Scarcity and availability were coordinated parts of audience management, rather than competing explanations for the act's reach.
- The nine-year listener shows fandom performing maintenance after label spending stopped. Keeping the fan club alive preserved attachment without Swiftwing funding the labor. Part of the apparatus had effectively been outsourced to the audience for free.
- **Landscape framing, not a new rule:** music without musicians and formula-written broadcast entertainment are already unremarkable printed background in this world. An expert system generates music to order without musicians and saturates the commercial record stores and pay-for-play databases, and at least one popular NBS adventure series runs with no writing staff at all, correlating 110 years of scripts against audience surveys, sponsors' sales figures, and market-study parameters. See *Sources*: Shadowbeat, printed pp. 8 and 30 - lore and vocabulary only, never SR3 rules. A singing database is not the scandal in Nashville. **Procurement is:** why pay to build a person when a database does the same work cheaper?

## Open questions

Settled by the GM ruling of 2026-11-10: Swiftwing **bought** Cindy and used her as an audience-analysis and market-shaping product, including the paid Ale 8 endorsement. These are **not** open questions.

- Since Swiftwing bought rather than built, **who built her**, and did that builder's architecture reach Swiftwing through **Core 7 / Site 7** directly or through an intermediary?
- How much continuity exists between the Swiftwing Cindy and later Cindy/Darla manifestations?
- **Settled by the GM ruling of 2026-11-16:** the endorsement work did extend beyond beverages. Cindy also recorded jingles for **SoyCorp Meatlets** and the **Augmented Beef and Bacon Social Club**, plus various unnamed local products, and every placement was both a viral marketing campaign and a subliminal influence operation. See [Cindy Lou Jenkins Back Catalog](../NPCs/Cindy-Lou-Jenkins-Back-Catalog.md).

- **Who paid for the persona apparatus.** The 2026-11-16 ruling puts a hugely expensive crowd campaign behind a thin concept. Whether it sat on Swiftwing's own books, was billed to a client, or was underwritten by a third party who wanted a crowd moved is open. The paid Ale 8 endorsement proves the client-funded model existed; it does not prove it funded her.
- **Whether the apparatus still has operators.** Budgets, staff, and a reason to keep spending are what made the persona durable. Nothing establishes that any of it was switched off when the act stopped paying.
