# Session 2026-10-22

![Kurgan empties his shotgun into the rotting Shedim above the burning vans while Belmont crashes through the McDonald's front and Curtis runs the drone from Grandpa's captain's chair.](../assets/images/Session-2026-10-22-Shedim-Banished.png)

## Summary

**The crew finished the fight the previous session started. Kyron's Mana Ball and Kurgan's shotgun bolts drove the Shedim to nine of ten boxes of damage; Kurgan's final shot banished it. Kyron's assensing identified the entity as a shedim — a spirit type new to the world within the last few years — banished for one turn of the moon, roughly thirty days, and not permanently dead. The crew then closed out the run: Kyron kept a possessed corpse as a potential ritual link, Buzz's footage of Lula Bell Sandusky exploding and the spirit emerging became the crown jewel of the evidence file, Cindy Lou wiped the restaurant's security footage, and the crew sold the package to Mayor Haggar's office through Joseph Neumann for 12,000¥ each plus 8 Karma each. A bid to sell the same story to Princeps was refused hostilely. One captured guard, one dead by Curtis's mini-tank, and a very confused Humanis thug chained to a chair at Taco's compound.**

Play resumed at immediate initiative inside the McDonald's parking lot from [Session 2026-10-15](2026-10-15.md). Before the dice turned over, the table spent roughly fifteen minutes repairing Cindy Lou: her session recap had been generated against the wrong week's transcript, transcript semantic search was returning unavailable, and text-to-speech was returning 403. Clint fixed the search and the Kokoro voice bridge in the terminal thread, and the GM restarted the voice session.

Combat resolved with the crew holding the tactical advantage almost throughout. Kilimanjaro's burst fire killed an unwounded guard outright. The Shedim raised the three downed bodies as possessed zombies. Kyron, the only witness with astral perception, identified them as reanimated and later identified the spirit itself. Kurgan's shotgun bolts registered as Moderate wounds despite the spirit's astral protection, and Kyron's Force 4, Moderate Mana Ball tied the spirit's defense for another Moderate wound. Curtis spent 2 Karma (the GM added 2 more) to throw off the lingering compulsion Lula Bell Sandusky had laid on him, then deployed Belmont through the restaurant wall on top of two security guards. The remaining living guards broke and fled; the fight effectively ended when Kurgan's last shotgun pass put the spirit over the edge and it faded out.

The important information came from Kyron's checks rather than the gunfire. The spirit was not a spirit of man and not a nature spirit: it was a shedim, a type that appeared in the Sixth World only a couple of years ago, canonically introduced in *Year of the Comet*. Banishment sends it home for one turn of the moon — about thirty days — unable to return under any circumstance, but it is not death. Kyron's later successful research established the only reliable permanent kill: open a portal to the shedim's home metaplane and destroy it there, a major magical undertaking. Spirits are not omniscient, and without a ritual link the banished entity has no more idea who shot it than the crew has of its name. That made the corpse valuable, and Kyron dragged one possessed zombie into Grandpa's under-floor storage, later into the compound's deep freeze.

The crew then behaved like professionals with a hangry member. Mevin's Police Procedures check surfaced the panic button behind the counter and bought them a vanishingly short window. Kyron's astral sweep proved the astral portal that had let the crew see and attack Sandusky sealed itself when she was banished, and that fire had destroyed most recoverable magical material. Cindy Lou, invoked by name, rolled Computer against target number 4 and wiped the restaurant's host "without too much difficulty." Buzz's pre-existing surveillance posture — the GM's worst possible successful roll of the night — produced the video of a beloved civic commentator exploding and a monster stepping out of her. That single file raised the price.

The crew sold. Curtis argued successfully for handing over the transformation footage, partly on the genuine grounds that the mayor deserved a warning. Mevin's drugged phone call to a Princeps contact was refused by someone who had expected to get the material for free; Curtis anonymized the signal. The mayor's side paid the 12,000¥-each figure the GM recalled as the original contract's 60,000¥ total, and everyone took 8 Karma. Kilimanjaro, unable to spend his share, hid it for a future character to find.

The GM's supplied table-time estimate was about **8:00-10:00 p.m. Central**. The capture spans **8:52 p.m. to 11:02 p.m. Eastern (about 7:52-10:02 p.m. Central)**, including pre-session troubleshooting and the post-game wrap.

## Major Scenes

- **Voice-bridge triage before initiative:** Cindy Lou's recap had been built from the wrong session twice and had also run long enough to be three clips played back to back. TTS returned 403 and transcript semantic search was unavailable. Clint verified and repaired the semantic search and the Kokoro voice bridge; the GM restarted the voice session, and the recap was ultimately superseded by the GM's own verbal recap of the McDonald's ambush.
- **Initiative and opening fire:** Kurgan 27, Kilimanjaro 20, Kyron 9, Curtis 7, Mevin 6. Kurgan held his first action. Kilimanjaro strafed toward the drive-through under covering burst fire (assault rifle, target number 5 after smartlink, movement, and explosion modifiers) and killed a living security guard outright — no resistance successes against a 12S burst.
- **The dead rise:** Kyron's first magic-background check failed; he recognized only "a spirit" and an off-putting rift in space. The spirit raised its arms and the three downed figures stood. A second check with two successes told Kyron all three had been dead or near death and were now dual-nature, something else wearing them.
- **Choosing the right spell:** The GM offered banishing versus damaging fire, warning that banishing something of that Force was likely to be hard. Kyron's magic-background check said the same thing without saying it: suspect banishment would be difficult. The GM flagged Mana Bolt as the cheap, low-drain, spirit-only option and explained the Force/damage-level split (light 1, moderate 3, serious 6, deadly 10) with drain scaling the same way. Kyron cast Mana Ball at Force 4, Moderate: sorcery 5 plus 4 spell dice, eight dice versus target number 4, six successes — and the spirit's own six successes tied him, for a Moderate wound. The GM emphasized that mana damage to a spirit does not simply regenerate the way ordinary damage does.
- **Curtis clears his head and lets the tank out:** Curtis rolled Willpower 5 against 6, spent 2 Karma, and had 2 more added by the GM. His willingness to overlook Sandusky's obviously lethal intentions drained away, leaving him lucid in front of fire, shambling corpses, and an angry wounded spirit. He then climbed into Grandpa's captain's chair, deployed Belmont down the rear ramp, and drove the Doberman through the front of the McDonald's, past a Coke machine, and in front of two slack-jawed guards.
- **The guards quit:** No loyalty among thieves. The outside guard who had not seen Kurgan's shot ran for a tree; the two inside broke for the far door after the mini-tank made its entrance, which spent Curtis's actions for the turn. Mevin made his Body check through the pain penalty, got up, and went prone behind the service counter.
- **Zombies vs. an armored RV:** The two remaining zombies took their turn pounding on Grandpa and accomplished nothing against the new armor, while Curtis watched through drone sensors and Buzz stayed deployed.
- **Shotgun against the astral:** Kurgan asked the GM, in character, whether Kyron's mana strike had looked effective, and was told it had seemed to land. He then shot the spirit at target number 2 with nine successes and 10S damage. The slug passed through a shimmering veil between two worlds that dissipated part of it — astral protection — and mundane-weapon protection further reduced the code, but the successes still stacked up into a second Moderate wound.
- **The grenade that ended the van:** Kilimanjaro switched to the underslung grenade launcher, rolling the associated Assault Rifle skill: six dice plus five saved from his combat pool against target number 4 for five successes. The security van failed its resistance and burst into flames and exploded, which killed one zombie outright, rendered the already-down one into pieces, and left the third wounded. The spirit reached nine of ten boxes of damage.
- **A bad-tempered spirit misses:** The GM stated plainly that "killing" a spirit banishes it rather than destroying it. The spirit then rolled high enough to refuse to leave out of pure rage, attacked at a plus-three target number for its accumulated wounds, and threw a bolt of black energy at Kilimanjaro that splashed against a wall, dissolved brick, and smelled like decay while hitting nothing. Cover would have helped, but the spirit's penalty meant it rolled no successes regardless.
- **The last shot:** Kurgan fired again at target number 2 against a stationary, badly wounded target and rolled all successes. The spirit faded.
- **Kyron names the thing:** Three successes on an Intelligence check told Kyron what the others could not see. He recognized the banishment signature from weaker spirits — the entity losing its anchor to the astral plane between the metaplanes and the physical plane — and understood it would be unable to return for one turn of the moon. He also placed it: not a spirit of man, not a nature spirit, but a completely different kind of spirit returning to a hostile metaplane that had only manifested a few years ago. A shedim.
- **The chase and the executions:** Kurgan ran down one fleeing guard a block or two away, winning an opposed Athletics roll by a single margin, and Kilimanjaro arrived close behind. Kurgan's Street Etiquette check said the man was genuine: a Humanis thug, openly prejudiced against magic, visibly disturbed at having spent hours in a vehicle with something like that. The crew knocked him out rather than killing him, planning to question him later. Curtis, meanwhile, cold-bloodedly cut down the two other fleeing guards with Belmont's machine guns because they had not been loaded with stun grenades. The GM marked it plainly: killed for the crime of not knowing their boss was a secret monster.
- **What is worth keeping:** Kyron projected around the site (Intelligence versus 5, three successes) and learned the astral portal that had let the crew perceive and attack Sandusky sealed itself the moment she was banished; fire had destroyed most recoverable magical material, leaving the possessed corpses as the only objects of real value. The GM then prompted the relatively new player directly: a mage would sweep for anomalous magical material or a possessed body for study. Kyron took the corpse. Curtis approved putting a cadaver in the RV's under-floor storage — "you're the mage, so if that's something we need, then sure" — and the body went into the compound's deep freeze alongside a previously frozen specimen the table half-remembered as a horse.
- **Buzz's worst-best roll of the night:** The GM rolled two dice against target number 2 for the surveillance drone — the target number being low precisely because surveillance is Buzz's job — and rolled the one and the two, the worst possible successful result. Buzz had been set up capturing distance surveillance and had footage of a do-gooder political commentator exploding in front of her security guards, with a monster emerging from her body. Kilimanjaro's response: "Our price just went up."
- **Cindy Lou wipes the host:** Kurgan asked Mevin to deck into the McDonald's host and wipe security footage before police arrived; Mevin, in pain and out of patience, asked Kilimanjaro to do it. The GM invoked Cindy Lou by name for a Computer check against target number 4, waited out a transfer, then reported that the robot handled it without too much difficulty. The table's transcript of the moment includes Cindy's own observation that the voice bridge rebooted mid-exchange.
- **The 30-day problem, and the only real answer:** Kurgan pressed Kyron on whether banishment meant permanence. The GM honored the earlier failed rolls rather than waving them through: Kyron knew shedim were new and poorly understood, knew the dead-body association, and could not say whether "Lula Bell Sandusky" had ever been a person who was taken over or had always been the spirit. Kurgan compared it to the insect-spirit infiltration the crew had seen before; Kyron, lacking the roll, could only say it sounded like the same pattern without certainty. Curtis asked the operational version — how do we keep it from coming back and killing us in thirty days? Kyron's successful research produced the answer later repeated as a voice line: banishment is a vacation, not a funeral. Break whatever anchors it here, and if you want it truly dead, open a portal to its home metaplane and kill it where it lives.
- **Who to sell, and who said no:** The crew reasoned that the information had shelf life only if the entity could rebuild a physical shell, and decided to sell quickly either way. Curtis pushed for handing over the transformation footage, both for value and because he genuinely wanted the mayor warned. Mevin's Princeps call — conducted through Curtis's burner-phone drawer, corporate etiquette versus 5 and 10 while drugged, Curtis rolling Electronics to anonymize the signal — was denied by a contact who showed open hostility toward someone they expected to hand the material over for free. On the mayor's side, the crew confirmed good standing: Mevin's MetroPedy relationship through Eric, Glenn now working with the Hidden Enforcers, and Kurgan building a fixer relationship with the mayor's office ahead of retirement.
- **Getting paid:** Everyone took **12,000¥** and **8 Karma**. Kilimanjaro could not spend his share, so he hid it where a future character might find it, and Kurgan noted it would not be the first time one character muled money to another.
- **Table cleanup:** The GM saved and closed the Tabletop Simulator client, and confirmed the next session as **Thursday, October 29**. Curtis remains stuck in a hotel room in Troy, Michigan, with a work laptop and an unresponsive TV, and asked the GM not to build maps for his sake.

## Clues Gained

- **The entity is a shedim, not a spirit of man.** New to the Sixth World within roughly the last couple of years, canonically introduced in *Year of the Comet*. Not well understood; Kyron's rolls to pin down its nature mostly failed.
- **Banishment is temporary.** One turn of the moon, roughly thirty days, unable to return under any circumstance. Repeatedly killed-and-banished is not the same as dead.
- **The only reliable permanent kill** is to open a portal to its home metaplane and destroy it there — a major magical undertaking, and beyond the crew's current means without significant resources.
- **Shedim are associated with dead bodies and ritual links.** Kyron's successful research said a possessed corpse can serve as a ritual link, permitting back-tracing and an advantage against a higher-level spirit. This is why the crew has a zombie in a freezer.
- **The shedim does not know who the crew is.** Without a ritual link it has no more information about them than they have about it. Its memory of the attack is, in the GM's phrasing, "some shadowy randos shot grenades at me till I died."
- **"Lula Bell Sandusky" may never have been a person.** Kyron's best-supported reading is that the spirit was the woman, but the GM explicitly kept this speculative pending deeper research.
- **Buzz recorded the transformation.** Video of Sandusky exploding and the monster emerging now exists, is preserved, and was handed to the mayor's office.
- **The McDonald's security host was wiped** by Cindy Lou before police could arrive, on a Computer check against target number 4.
- **The captured guard is Humanis-affiliated** and was genuinely disturbed by what he had been sitting next to. His testimony about any change in Sandusky's demeanor over time is unexploited.
- **The evidence sold**, which means the crew's copies, the footage, and the paydata are the remaining leverage. The original Johnson was never contacted again and remains unidentified.

## Decisions Made

- Fight rather than attempt banishment, on Kyron's own assessment that banishing something of that Force would likely fail.
- Keep a possessed corpse for magical study and as a possible ritual link, over Curtis's mild objection and with his permission.
- Knock out rather than execute the guard Kurgan caught; execute the two who fled with Belmont.
- Wipe the McDonald's host before police response, rather than rely on speed.
- Regroup at Taco's compound before selling, prioritizing rest, containment, and Mevin's painkillers.
- Sell to Mayor Haggar's office through Joseph Neumann, including the transformation footage, rather than holding it; decline to pursue the Princeps angle after being refused.
- Do not attempt to prepare for the spirit's return within thirty days, on the reasoning that it has no way to identify the crew and will likely not return to the Sandusky identity once that identity is burned.
